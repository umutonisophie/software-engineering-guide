Software Engineering

This site introduces software engineering and walks through the ten specialisations that make up the field. Each page covers one area: what the work involves, what makes it difficult, and what it leads to.

What is software engineering?

It is the job of designing, developing, testing and maintaining software systems. The word that matters is engineering. Writing code that works once is easy; building something that keeps working as requirements change, as users discover it is wrong, and as other people maintain it after you have moved on, is the discipline.

The ten specialisations

They are listed in the sidebar. Some are implementation roles (frontend, mobile, IoT), some are enabling ones (Python, QA, security), and some decide what gets built rather than how (product design, product management, UX research). Most working engineers draw on more than one, because the boundaries are drawn for teaching convenience rather than by the work itself.

Choosing a direction

Pick the area whose problems sound interesting to you, not the one with the most job listings. Motivation is what carries someone through the parts of a field that are genuinely tedious, and every one of these has tedious parts. It is also the cheapest thing to get wrong: a month reading about a field will tell you more than a year of studying it.

Where to start

Begin with the introduction, then read the two specialisations closest to what you already know, and the two furthest from it. The contrast is instructive.
