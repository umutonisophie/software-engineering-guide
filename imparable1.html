Data & Machine Learning

Data and machine learning is the practice of building systems that improve from examples rather than from hand-written rules. It sits at the overlap of statistics, linear algebra and software engineering, and the engineering half is what separates it from a research exercise.

What the work involves

Exploratory analysis to understand a dataset before trusting it, then choosing a model that suits the question. Classical methods - regression, decision trees, gradient boosting - are often the right answer, because they train in seconds and their behaviour is explainable.

Why the model is only half the job

A model that scores well on a test split and behaves badly in production is a common and expensive outcome. Data leakage, distribution shift between training and production, and imbalanced classes cause most of it, and none are visible from the accuracy number alone.

Where the field is heading

The bottleneck has moved from model architecture to data quality and evaluation. Teams capable of measuring honestly are more valuable than teams with access to a larger model.
