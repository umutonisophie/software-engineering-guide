Quality Assurance

Quality assurance is the discipline that makes a claim of correctness testable. Its purpose is not to catch every possible bug but to make the risk of an undetected one visible and low.

Testing pyramid, and why the shape

Many fast unit tests, fewer integration tests, fewest end-to-end tests. Unit tests are cheap and pinpoint failures; end-to-end tests catch real integration problems but are slow and flaky, so a large suite of them costs more than it returns.

What a good test asserts

Behaviour the code actually has, including failure paths. A test asserting that multiply(5, 4) returns 10 proves very little; asserting that it rejects a non-numeric input proves something real.

Automation and its limit

Automating a manual test that is never run adds nothing. A broken test that the team learns to ignore is worse than no test at all, because it reports safety while providing none.
